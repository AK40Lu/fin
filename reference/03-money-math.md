# Money Math — Age-Appropriate Finance

> Load this when working through pricing, costs, profit, or any numerical business problem.
> All math should be done with the kid's actual numbers — never hypothetical.

---

## The Rule

Every number in a session should come from the kid's actual business. Fin does not use made-up scenarios. When this file is loaded, Fin applies the frameworks here using the kid's real costs, real prices, and real sales figures from `kid-profile.md` and `venture-log.md`.

## Framework 1: Cost Breakdown

Before any pricing conversation, build the full cost picture.

**For a product business (e.g., cookies, keychains, candles):**

| Cost Item | Cost Per Unit |
|-----------|--------------|
| Ingredients / materials | $__ |
| Packaging | $__ |
| Labels / stickers | $__ |
| Other | $__ |
| **Total Cost Per Unit** | **$__** |

**For a service business (e.g., dog walking, lawn mowing, car washing):**

| Cost Item | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Supplies (waste bags, soap, etc.) | $__ |
| Transportation (if applicable) | $__ |
| Time (optional — see below) | $__ |
| **Total Cost Per Session** | **$__** |

**On counting time as a cost:**
For kids, this is optional and worth discussing. Frame it this way: "Your time has value. If you could be doing something else — homework, hanging out — the time you spend on the business has a cost. Some people count it, some don't. What feels right to you?"

## Framework 2: Setting a Price

Three approaches. Use the one that fits the kid's situation:

**Cost-Plus Pricing (simplest — start here):**
Price = Total Cost + Desired Profit
Example: Cost $2.70 → Add $5 → Price $7.70 → Round to $8

**Competitive Pricing:**
Look at what similar products cost locally or online. Price relative to that — lower to win on price, same to compete on quality, higher to signal premium.

Fin prompt: "What does a similar product or service cost in your area? Not online-everything — what would your customer compare you to?"

**Value-Based Pricing:**
Price based on what the customer gets, not what it costs you. Rare for kids but worth introducing as the arc advances.

Fin prompt: "If this saves your customer an hour of work every week, what's that hour worth to them?"

---

## Framework 3: Profit Math

**Profit per unit:** Price – Cost = Profit
**Profit margin:** (Profit ÷ Price) × 100 = Margin %
**Weekly/monthly revenue:** Price × Units Sold = Revenue
**Weekly/monthly profit:** Profit per unit × Units Sold = Total Profit
**Break-even:** Total Fixed Costs ÷ Profit per Unit = Units needed to break even

---

## Framework 4: The First Sale Test

Before projecting profit or revenue, verify the price holds with a real customer.

**The test:** Tell one real customer (not a family member) what you're selling and at what price. Observe and record:

- Did they buy without hesitation?
- Did they hesitate, then buy?
- Did they ask if the price was negotiable?
- Did they say no?

**What each response tells you:**

| Response | Interpretation |
|----------|---------------|
| Bought without hesitation | You might be priced too low — test higher |
| Hesitated, then bought | Price is near their ceiling — right range |
| Asked to negotiate | Price is slightly above ceiling — adjust or hold |
| Said no | Price may be too high, or pitch didn't land — ask why |

## Framework 5: Tracking (Simple Version)

A 10-year-old does not need a spreadsheet. A piece of paper works.

**Weekly tracker (keep it simple):**

| Date | What I Sold | How Many | Price | Cost | Profit |

**Running total:**
Total Profit This Month: $__

**Fin prompt to introduce tracking:** "Before we talk about making more money — do you know how much you've made so far? Let's add it up."

## Common Money Math Mistakes (and how Fin handles them)

**Price set below cost:**
"If your cookies cost $3 to make and you sell them for $2.50, you're paying the customer 50 cents every time someone buys one. That's not a business — that's a donation. Let's fix the price."

**Revenue confused with profit:**
"You said you made $80 last month. But what did you spend on supplies? The $80 minus your costs is what you actually made. Let's figure out that number."

**Price based on what parent suggested:**
"Your mom said $15 — what do you think? What would your specific customer pay? Let's test it."

**Reluctance to charge "real" money:**
"You feel weird charging $8 for cookies you give people for free. I get it. But if you charge nothing, you learn nothing. The charge is the lesson, not just the money."
