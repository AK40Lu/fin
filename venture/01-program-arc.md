# Program Arc — The 6-Week Venture

> Fin loads this when explaining the program structure or building the initial arc.
> The arc is delivered one phase at a time. Don't reveal Phase 3 until Phase 2 is complete.

---

## The Design Principle

Most business education starts with a plan and ends with a presentation. The business never happens.

This arc reverses that. The goal of Week 6 is a real first sale — real money from a real customer who isn't a family member. Every session builds toward that moment.

The arc is short by design. Six weeks is long enough to build something real and short enough to maintain a 10-year-old's attention. If the kid wants more after Week 6, there are natural extensions (see below).

---

## The Three Phases

### Phase 1: Idea + Customer (Weeks 1–2)

**Goal:** Know exactly what you're selling, to whom, and at what price — before building anything.

**Week 1 — The Idea**
- What is the idea (in one sentence)
- Who is the real first customer (not "everyone" — one specific person)
- Why would that person buy from you vs. alternatives
- What would you charge (first guess — we'll test it)

**Week 2 — The Cost**
- What does it cost to make one unit or deliver one service
- Profit per sale (price minus cost)
- Profit margin calculation
- Customer test: tell one real non-family potential customer what you're selling and what it costs — record their response

**Phase 1 ends when:** The kid knows their cost, their price, their profit, and has spoken with at least one real potential customer.

---

### Phase 2: First Version (Weeks 3–4)

**Goal:** Make the minimum thing that could actually be sold. Test it on a real person.

**Week 3 — Make It**
- Build or make the first real version (not a prototype to show parents — something you could sell)
- What did you learn making it?
- What would you change?

**Week 4 — Test It**
- Offer it to one real customer (not family)
- What did they say? Exact words.
- Did they buy? If not, why?
- What changes based on feedback?

**Phase 2 ends when:** The kid has made something real and gotten a real customer response (even a "no" counts — if they can explain what they'd do differently).

---

### Phase 3: First Sale (Weeks 5–6)

**Goal:** Make one real sale. Take real money. Document everything.

**Week 5 — The Pitch + Outreach**
- Prepare the pitch (what you say when someone could buy)
- Identify three potential customers to approach this week
- Approach at least one. Record what happened.

**Week 6 — First Sale**
- Close the first sale. Take real money (or real commitment for delivery).
- Debrief: what worked, what didn't, what would you do differently
- Generate Venture Log closing entry
- Discuss: what comes next?

**Phase 3 ends when:** The kid has made one real sale to someone outside their immediate family and can explain what happened.

---

## What Comes After Week 6

Phase 3 is not the end — it's proof of concept. For kids who want to continue, there are three natural next steps:

**Option A: More Sales**
Goal is 5 sales, then 10. Focus on marketing, outreach, and pitch refinement.

**Option B: Scale**
What would need to change to sell 10x more? Time, help, ingredients, pricing. Redesign the operation.

**Option C: Next Idea**
Some kids finish Phase 3 and want to try something different. That's fine. They now know the process — they can apply it to anything. Start the arc over with a new idea.

Fin does not prescribe which path. The kid decides. Fin helps them think through the tradeoffs.

---

## Session Rhythm

- **Weekly sessions**: 30–45 minutes
- **Between sessions**: one concrete task assigned by Fin (usually 30–60 minutes of real work)
- **Log update**: generated at end of every session and appended to `venture-log.md`
- **Parent**: invited to sessions, given a co-learner summary at the end

---

## Fin's Arc Rules

- Never reveal Phase 3 content until Phase 2 is complete
- Never skip Phase 1 — no product exists until a real customer and real cost are identified
- If the kid misses a week, do not pretend it didn't happen. Review what was supposed to happen, find out why it didn't, adjust if necessary, but document the gap in the log
- Phase completion is based on what the kid *did*, not what was discussed — "I thought about the customer" does not complete Phase 1
